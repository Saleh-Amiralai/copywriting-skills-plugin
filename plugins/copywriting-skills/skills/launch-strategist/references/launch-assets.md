# Launch Asset Templates

Templates and structure for the actual pieces of a launch. Open this when the user needs a specific asset written, not just strategy.

## Launch email anatomy

Every launch email needs:
- **Subject line** — trigger language, one job only: get the open. Don't try to sell in the subject line.
- **Opening hook** — the first sentence must earn the second one. Skip pleasantries ("I hope this finds you well"); open with something that creates curiosity or names the reader's pain directly.
- **One core idea** — a single focus per email (the offer, a transformation story, one objection, social proof — never several at once).
- **Explicit CTA** — tell the reader exactly what to do next, repeated at beginning/middle/end for longer sales emails since many readers skim straight to the end.
- **A P.S.** — reinforces the CTA or adds urgency; often the first or last thing a skimmer actually reads.

Writing tips: write conversationally (contractions, sentence fragments, starting with "And"/"But" if it flows) — never a corporate press-release voice. Use stories over bare claims. Frame around the reader ("You're about to discover..." beats "I'm excited to share..."). Address objections directly rather than hoping they don't come up. Keep it to 300-600 words — launch emails don't need to be long, just focused.

## Proven 7-day / 11-email open-cart sequence skeleton

| Email | Timing | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Event day, immediately after | Replay + full offer details |
| 2 | Event day, evening | Early-bird bonus reminder (if applicable) |
| 3 | Day 2 | Transformation email — paint the after picture |
| 4 | Day 3 | Objection handling (time or fit) |
| 5 | Day 4 | Social proof — testimonials/case studies |
| 6 | Day 5 | Money objection / value justification |
| 7 | Day 6 | 24-hour warning + FAQ |
| 8 | Day 7, morning | "Today's the last day" |
| 9 | Day 7, midday | "If you're on the fence..." |
| 10 | Day 7, evening | "Final hours" |
| 11 | Day 7, late | "Cart closes in 1 hour" |

Eleven emails in seven days sounds like a lot, but each one carries a different angle — it's not repetition, it's addressing different hesitations from different people at different points in their decision.

## Webinar registration page

One goal only: get the registration. Keep the whole page to one or two scrolls — this is a free training, not the $3,000 program, so make saying yes effortless.

- **Headline** — specific and benefit-driven, not a generic label: "How to Launch Your Course in 30 Days (Even If You Don't Have an Audience Yet)," not "Free Webinar on Course Creation."
- **Subheadline** — adds specificity or preempts an objection ("The exact step-by-step process I used to go from zero to $10K in my first launch").
- **3-5 bullets** — specific things they'll learn or walk away with, not vague topic labels.
- **Registration form** — name and email only. Every extra field costs registrations.
- **Social proof** — testimonials from past attendees, if this event has run before.
- **Honest urgency** — only if real: a bonus for the first N registrants, for example.
- **Date/time** — crystal clear, in multiple time zones if the audience is spread out.

## Sales page structure

Long-form (3,000-5,000+ words) converts better for high-ticket offers because bigger decisions need more information to justify; offers under $500 can run shorter (1,500-2,000 words). Regardless of length: short paragraphs, bullets, bold text, generous white space, an "Enroll Now" CTA every 1-2 scrolls, and proof over bare claims throughout.

Section order:
1. **Above the fold** — headline via the Bridge Framework, subheadline, CTA button, hero image or video.
2. **Pain points** — 3-5 specific, visceral descriptions of the current struggle, written so the reader thinks "that's exactly what I'm going through."
3. **Transformation** — the vivid after-picture; what life looks like once the problem is solved.
4. **Your story** — how the solution was discovered; builds relatability, kept tight and focused on the transformation, not a full biography.
5. **The solution (the program)** — introduced via the Bridge Framework, named, with the unique methodology explained.
6. **What's included** — module by module, each one framed by the transformation it creates, not just its topic: "Module 1: The Email Foundation — build a list that actually buys. You'll learn the lead magnet formula 95% of creators get wrong..." rather than "Module 1: Email Marketing Basics."
7. **Bonuses** — each with a standalone value, chosen because they remove a real obstacle to implementation.
8. **Value stack** — Total Value vs. Your Investment vs. You Save, shown visually.
9. **Social proof** — 5-10 testimonials, mixed formats (written with photos, video, screenshots, before/after case studies with real numbers).
10. **About you** — credentials and results that build trust, tied back to how they benefit the reader.
11. **FAQ** — every real objection, answered specifically (time, fit, experience level, audience size, refunds, access length).
12. **Guarantee** — stated plainly to reduce perceived risk.
13. **Final CTA** — with real urgency (the actual cart-close time and what happens after).

## Webinar slide deck outline

**Intro (5-8 slides):** title → proof/hook (lead with the strongest result, don't bury it) → quick social proof (3-4 flashed testimonials) → agenda ("today you'll discover...") → housekeeping (stay to the end, notebook, Q&A at the end) → who this is for.

**Teaching (15-25 slides, ~5-8 per point, 3 points total):** title naming the mistake/belief → the problem → why it's wrong (data, story, or logic) → the better approach → a quick, implementable win → transition to the next point.

**Transition (2-3 slides):** acknowledge the gap between what was just taught and full implementation → introduce the program by name → establish credibility (results, experience, numbers).

**Pitch (15-20 slides):** WHO / BEFORE / AFTER / HOW via the Bridge Framework → program overview → one slide per module, each framed by transformation not topic → bonuses → value stack (Total Value vs. Investment) → who this is for *and* who it's not for → guarantee → urgency/scarcity (real) → FAQ (3-5 objections) → CTA with the enrollment link visible on the slide → thank you.

**Design principles:** one idea per slide (a slide with seven bullets should become seven slides), large fonts (30pt+), real photos over stock images, consistent styling throughout, and a dry-run with another person before going live to catch anything confusing.

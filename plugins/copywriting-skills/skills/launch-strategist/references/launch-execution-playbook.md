# Launch Execution Playbook

Day-by-day and phase-by-phase tactics. Use this once the overall strategy (format, timeline, framework) is set and it's time to plan or write the actual week-by-week execution.

## Pre-launch (3-4 weeks before cart opens)

Pre-launch has exactly three jobs: make the audience acutely aware of their problem, show that solving it is both urgent and possible, and position the launcher as the obvious guide. It does **not** mention the offer yet.

**Week-by-week shape:**
- Week 1: problem awareness and agitation.
- Week 2: possibility and transformation — show that change is achievable.
- Week 3: introduce the methodology, build anticipation.
- Week 4 (or the tail of week 3): shift to directly promoting the conversion event.

**Content types that do the work:**
- **Myth-busting** — challenge a belief the audience currently holds ("You've been told posting daily drives sales. It's actually killing your engagement. Here's why.").
- **Transformation stories** — yours or a client's, with before/after *specificity* ("60-hour weeks at $5K/month → 25-hour weeks at $20K/month" beats "things got better").
- **Pain-point content** — make the cost of inaction concrete and uncomfortable (a dollar figure, a time figure).
- **Behind-the-scenes** — tease what's coming without revealing it (the Zeigarnik effect: unfinished threads stick in memory).
- **Social proof content** — results from the methodology in general, even before the specific offer is named.
- **Mini-training** — a genuinely useful short lesson that also showcases teaching style and voice, pre-qualifying future buyers.

**Cadence:** email 2-4x/week, 200-400 words each, one idea per email. Subject lines should use trigger language, not tip language — "Why your launches keep flopping (and what winners do differently)," not "How to improve your launch results."

**Pre-launch survey** (2-3 weeks out): 3 short questions — biggest struggle, what would make this an easy yes, what's stopped them from solving it already. This sharpens messaging, gets people mentally engaged with their own problem, and lets later emails say "many of you told me..." which makes respondents feel heard.

**The countdown (final 5-7 days):** shift explicitly to promoting the conversion event. A proven promotion cadence:
- 7 days before: announcement email
- 5 days before: "here's what we'll cover"
- 3 days before: "why you should attend live"
- 1 day before: last chance to register
- Day of: "we start in a few hours," plus 2-3 same-day reminders (morning, 2-hours-out, 15-minutes-out)

People don't register for generic "trainings" — they register for a specific promised outcome, so every registration-push email should restate the specific thing they'll walk away with.

## Conversion event (webinar, ~60-75 minutes)

The event has two jobs — teach genuinely and pitch convincingly — and most failed webinars only do one of them.

1. **Introduction (5-10 min).** The first three minutes decide whether people stay. Hook with the strongest result or promise immediately; save the personal story for later.
2. **Teaching (35-45 min), three points.** For each: name the mistake/limiting belief → explain why it's wrong → show the better approach → give a quick, actionable win → transition to the next point. Teaching should create real "aha" moments while making clear that full implementation is what the paid program is for — like a cooking show that teaches one dish without teaching culinary school.
3. **Transition (3-5 min).** Acknowledge what's just been taught is genuinely useful, then name the gap ("implementing this alone can be overwhelming..."). This is also where credibility gets established — results, experience, numbers — briefly, not a full biography.
4. **The pitch (15-20 min).** Walk the Bridge Framework (WHO → BEFORE → AFTER → HOW → WHEN), then the offer itself — module-by-module transformation, not a feature list — then bonuses and the value stack, pre-empting the top objections along the way.
5. **The close (3-5 min).** One explicit CTA, with real urgency (cart timing, a genuine price increase, or a bonus expiration).
6. **Q&A (10-15 min, if live).** Gold for surfacing unaddressed objections. Common questions worth having ready answers for: time commitment, "what if I'm a beginner," payment plans, bonus details.

Send the replay immediately to everyone who registered (attendees and no-shows alike), with the purchase link and the real cart-closing time front and center.

**Format alternatives**, one line each:
- **Challenge** — pitch lands on the final day, after participants have had a few days of small wins and momentum.
- **Workshop** — shorter (30-45 min), hands-on; the pitch is "you just built X — imagine the full program."
- **Video series** — pitch in the final video; combat binge-watching/drop-off with email nudges and a live Q&A add-on.
- **Masterclass** — a tighter 45-minute hybrid of webinar and workshop, good for shorter-attention audiences.

## Open cart (daily rhythm, minimum one email/day)

Expect a strong day one, a quiet middle (days 2-4 — this is normal, not failure), and a final-day surge. Each email needs one distinct angle:

| Day | Angle |
|---|---|
| 1 (event day) | Replay + full offer details |
| 2 | Transformation — paint the after picture in detail, ideally via a student story |
| 3 | Objection handling (usually time or fit) |
| 4 | Social proof — multiple testimonials/case studies |
| 5 | FAQ and remaining details |
| 6 | 24-hour warning |
| 7 (close) | Multiple emails — see Close Cart below |

Subject lines should keep using trigger language over tip language throughout — e.g. Day 3: "But I don't have time... (do you though?)" rather than "Common questions about \[Program\]."

**Social media during open cart:** daily posts minimum, mixing behind-the-scenes updates, student spotlights, public FAQ answers, countdown posts, and short live videos — variety matters more than volume; don't repeat the same "enrollment is open" message every day.

**Handling inbound questions:** respond fast (a same-day reply catches someone while they're still warm; a next-day reply often means they've already talked themselves out of it), personalize every response even if it's the tenth time answering the same question, and for higher-ticket offers, offer a short call to close out fence-sitters.

**Managing energy during open cart:** pre-write and schedule emails before the cart opens, batch social content in advance, set explicit work-hour boundaries, delegate what can be delegated, and track metrics daily so momentum (or the lack of it) is visible rather than just felt.

## Close cart (final day — this generates 30-50% of total launch revenue)

The spike happens because loss aversion, visible social proof, and a forced deadline converge in the final hours — trust it even if the days before felt slow.

| Time | Email | Tone |
|---|---|---|
| 8-9am | "Today's the last day" | Direct, substantial (400-600 words), full recap + CTA |
| 12-2pm | "If you're on the fence..." | Empathetic, cost-of-waiting framing, a real "almost didn't, then did" story |
| 6-8pm | "Final hours" | Short and punchy, bonus-expiry reminder, 2-3 strong testimonials |
| 10-11pm | "1 hour left" | Shortest (100-150 words), stark yes/no framing |
| After close | "The doors are closed" | Gratitude for who joined, graceful acknowledgment for who didn't, downsell/waitlist invite |

Stay highly visible on social throughout the day — live videos and frequent stories create the FOMO that the emails alone can't.

**Handling last-minute requests:** be flexible about *how* someone pays (alternate payment methods for a declined card), not about *when* — don't grant individual deadline extensions, since it teaches the whole list that deadlines don't really mean anything. Answer late-night questions quickly; someone messaging minutes before close is a hot lead. If a short universal grace period is used, apply it quietly and consistently, then actually close.

## Post-launch

- **Downsell** (sent 12-48 hours after close, to non-buyers): a smaller, more accessible offer for people who wanted in but couldn't at the main price or format. Can add another 10-20% on top of launch revenue.
- **"Why didn't you buy?" survey** (3-5 questions: primary reason, what would've made it an easy yes, biggest ongoing struggle, interest in a future opening) — the most direct source of intel for the next launch.
- **Onboarding new students**: an immediate welcome email with access instructions, the very first step to take, and ideally one unadvertised surprise bonus that reinforces they made the right call.
- **Debrief** (block 2-3 hours within the week after): review the numbers (list size, registrations/show-up rate, conversion rate, revenue/AOV, email open/click rates, ad ROI if applicable) alongside the qualitative read (what felt good or hard, recurring objections, what to change next time). Write it down — it will not be remembered by the next launch.
- **Rest and celebrate before planning the next move** — launches are genuinely exhausting, and skipping this step shows up as burnout by the next cycle.

# Repurposing and Voice Adaptation

## Turning one piece of content into many posts

The mistake to avoid is treating repurposing as "chop the long thing into shorter things." The better approach extracts standalone ideas from the source and rebuilds each one natively for its destination format.

**One Pillar, Many Cuts.** Start from one long-form "pillar" (a video, article, podcast episode, or keynote — anything over roughly 10 minutes or 1,500 words). Extract 30-50 micro-content moments from it: quotable lines, individual tips, short stories, data points, contrarian takes, before/after moments, Q&A exchanges. Format each one natively for its target platform rather than trimming the original wording, and spread them out across days or weeks rather than posting everything from one pillar at once.

**Content Atomization (hub-and-spoke).** Treat the pillar as the hub; every derivative post is a spoke. Pull out 5-15 "atoms" and tag each by type — stat, quote, insight, question, contrarian take, how-to, analogy, case study, prediction — then rate each for standalone impact. Contrarian atoms consistently rate highest for engagement potential; prioritize those when choosing what to post first.

**Content Waterfall (weekly cascade).** Spread derivative content across a week so it doesn't all compete for the same attention: a long-form pillar early in the week, a thread/carousel pairing mid-week, a visual quote-card post, a poll around midweek (a natural engagement peak), a newsletter later in the week, and lighter, more personal or community-oriented content toward the weekend. The rule underneath all of this: never post identical content to two platforms on the same day, and let high-effort formats breathe rather than clustering them.

**8-step repurposing system**, when working through a larger backlog of existing content: (1) audit what already exists, (2) identify the top-performing ~20% — that's the highest-leverage material to repurpose first, especially anything evergreen, (3) map each content type to the platform formats it suits (data-heavy material to threads/carousels/newsletters; story-driven material to carousels/community posts; opinion/contrarian material to LinkedIn/X text), (4) build a message map — one main claim, three supporting points, three proof items — that scales up or down depending on the destination format, (5) plan distribution alongside creation rather than as an afterthought, (6) use AI to accelerate drafting while a human still owns voice, nuance, and final approval, (7) track what actually performs and feed that back into future choices, (8) refresh evergreen pillars every 3-6 months rather than letting them go stale.

## Keeping voice consistent while tone shifts per platform

The core brand voice — the personality, the values, the way of seeing things — should stay constant across every platform. What changes is **tone**: formality, playfulness, directness. A witty brand should read as witty everywhere; it's the level of polish that flexes.

When establishing or checking a voice, five dimensions are useful to score (even informally, 1-5): **formality**, **humor**, **directness**, **emotion**, and **jargon**. Adjust these per platform rather than rewriting the underlying personality:

- **Instagram** — relaxed, visual, conversational. Emoji-rich (roughly 3-8 per caption) fits the platform norm. Contractions always. Avoid: formal language, jargon, and text-heavy captions with no visual breaks.
- **LinkedIn** — professional, first-person, opinion-led, a measured kind of authority. Emoji: none, or at most 1-2, used sparingly. Contractions are fine but less frequent than on more casual platforms. Short paragraphs (1-2 sentences) and real whitespace. Avoid: hashtag-stuffed openings, "Agree?"-style engagement bait, and corporate-sounding phrasing.
- **Facebook** — warm, community-focused, conversational, often ending in a question to invite comments. Emoji moderate (roughly 2-4 per post). Avoid: hard-sell CTAs and link-heavy posts that feel like an ad rather than a conversation. (There isn't a deep organic-strategy playbook to draw on here the way there is for Instagram and LinkedIn — treat this as a light tone guide, not a full format strategy.)

A simple way to make the tone-shift explicit when asked to write "in a more casual/professional/witty voice": **casual** means more emoji, more contractions, shorter fragments, and lower formality; **professional** means less emoji, more structure, more data-forward phrasing, higher formality; **witty** means personality-forward language, wordplay, and punchy one-liners, balanced so it doesn't undercut clarity.

## Mistakes that specifically show up when repurposing or cross-posting

- **Posting everything from a pillar on the same day.** It fatigues the audience and doesn't get rewarded by any platform's algorithm — stagger across roughly 5-7 days instead.
- **Wrong aspect ratios per platform.** Each platform has an exact expected format (see the Instagram and LinkedIn reference files); reusing one platform's crop on another causes awkward letterboxing or cropping that reads as careless.
- **Publishing without a final human pass.** AI-drafted voice is often detectable, and small tone or factual slips are easy to miss without a read-through before it goes out — always do one.
- **Ignoring the reply/comment strategy.** Especially on LinkedIn, a substantive comment (10+ words) is a much stronger engagement signal than a like — plan a few genuine reply prompts or seed the first comment rather than treating publishing as the finish line.

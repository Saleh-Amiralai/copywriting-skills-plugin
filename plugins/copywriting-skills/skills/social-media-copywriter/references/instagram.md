# Instagram

## What the algorithm actually rewards

Instagram's ranking centers on watch time, likes, and sends — but which one matters depends on who's seeing the post. For reach among existing followers, optimize for **likes per reach**. For reach beyond followers (the harder, more valuable kind), optimize for **sends per reach** — content has to be "sendable" to a specific person, not just generically good. A post that makes someone think "X needs to see this" outperforms one that's merely well-liked.

Original content is increasingly favored over reposts — accounts that mostly repost other creators' content (especially with visible watermarks from other apps) get throttled. Muted Reels without captions are also flagged as a quality risk, since so much viewing happens sound-off.

Hashtags carry much less discovery weight than they used to — Instagram can no longer be followed by hashtag. Caption and bio keyword relevance now matters more than hashtag volume for being found.

## Format specs

- **Reels**: 9:16 vertical (1080×1920). Organic Reels can run up to 3 minutes, but the real sweet spot for engagement is 7-30 seconds — short enough to loop and get replayed, which itself is a positive signal.
- **Feed**: portrait 4:5 (1080×1350) or square/carousel default 1:1 (1080×1080).
- **Carousels**: up to 20 slides. Music can be added. Keep the bottom/right edges clear on any video format — UI elements (captions, audio attribution, CTA controls) overlap there.
- **Caption**: 2,200-character ceiling, but the meaningful limit is the ~125 characters visible before "...more" — that's the real hook window.

## Content-type playbook

**Reels** average roughly double the reach of static posts and tend to drive the most impressions and interactions of any format. Hook in the first 1-3 seconds — open with the payoff, conflict, or result, not context-setting. Since many people watch muted, captions/text overlay aren't optional, they're load-bearing.

**Carousels** consistently post the highest engagement rate of any Instagram format, sometimes for a simple reason: they reward save-and-share behavior more than any other type. Treat the cover slide like a headline — it has to work as a hook completely on its own, since that's the only slide most people ever see before deciding whether to swipe. Listicles and how-tos ("mini-blog post" framing) tend to outperform, and the last slide should carry an explicit CTA.

**Stories** are a nurture tool, not a reach tool — they're shown mostly to people who already follow. Their real value is interactive stickers (polls, quizzes, question boxes), which both drive engagement and function as informal audience research or a soft lead-qualification step.

**Collab posts** (now supporting 3+ collaborators) show to the combined follower pools of everyone tagged, and tend to substantially outperform solo posts on reach — worth using whenever a genuine collaborator exists.

## Hook and caption structure

Two frameworks do most of the work:
- **Hook-Story-Offer** — a hook grabs attention, a short story builds connection or insight, the offer/CTA closes.
- **AIDA** — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action.

Effective hook types: bold/surprising statements, curiosity-driven questions, numbered lists ("5 ways to..."), relatable call-outs, and contrarian takes. Keep the actual hook line under 8-12 words and make sure it displays before the "...more" cutoff — everything after that point is a bonus, not guaranteed to be read.

Format the caption body for mobile scanning: short lines, breathing room, emoji as visual bullets where it fits the brand voice — not walls of text.

## CTAs

Five CTA types to choose from (pick one per post): **engagement** (like/save/tag), **traffic** (link in bio/link sticker), **direct communication** ("Comment X and I'll DM you Y" — a common lead-capture pattern), **conversion** (sale/lead-gen), and **community** (join a group/podcast/newsletter). A soft engagement CTA ("save this") can pair with a harder one ("link in bio") in the same post, but avoid stacking more than two asks. Start CTA lines with an action verb. Some creators place the actual link in their own first comment rather than the caption, to sidestep any link-related caption penalty.

## Hashtags

Discovery weight has dropped significantly — use 3-5 relevant hashtags (up to ~10 at most), not the old advice of maxing out at 30. Prioritize keyword-rich, natural-language captions and a keyword-optimized bio over hashtag volume.

## Profile and funnel structure

Treat the bio as a mini CTA: who the account helps, plus what to do next. Structure Highlights as funnel stages — Start Here → Freebies → Testimonials → Offer/Work With Me → FAQ — so a new visitor can self-navigate. Treat the link-in-bio destination as a compact landing page with one clear primary action, not a link dump.

## Visual consistency

A small, consistent palette (2-4 brand colors, 1-2 fonts) and a repeatable template system reads as professional without needing a design team — but don't over-polish at the expense of feeling real; Instagram audiences respond to authenticity as much as production value.

# Social Media Copywriter

This skill turns Claude into a social media copywriter across Instagram, LinkedIn, and short-form thread platforms (Threads/X). Each platform rewards different things — what stops a scroll on Instagram reads as spam on LinkedIn — so the right move is almost never to copy-paste one post everywhere. This skill picks the right structure for the platform and format, then writes to it.

## Before writing: get context

Ask for what's missing (skip what's already established in the conversation):

- **Platform and format** — Instagram (caption/carousel/Reel hook/Story), LinkedIn post, or a thread-style platform? Each has its own structure below.
- **Source material** — is this original, or repurposed from something longer (a video, podcast episode, blog post, newsletter)? If repurposing, see [references/repurposing-and-voice.md](references/repurposing-and-voice.md) for how to extract multiple posts from one piece without just chopping it up.
- **Brand voice** — don't default to any one tone. Ask (or infer from prior context/examples) whether the brand is bold/edgy, warm/empathetic, polished/professional, or something else — see [references/repurposing-and-voice.md](references/repurposing-and-voice.md) for how the *same* core voice should flex by platform.
- **Goal/CTA** — engagement (comments, saves, shares), traffic (link in bio), lead capture (DM funnel, "comment X for Y"), or direct conversion?
- **Proof on hand** — any real numbers, results, or stories available. Never fabricate a statistic or claim — ask for one, or write around its absence, rather than inventing it.

## Core principles across every platform

- **Mobile-first formatting.** Most consumption happens on a phone: short paragraphs (1-2 sentences), deliberate line breaks, no dense text blocks. This matters more than almost any other stylistic choice.
- **The hook has a hard fold.** Every platform truncates before a "see more" — write the single strongest line first, not a warm-up. Rough fold points: Instagram ~125 characters into the caption; LinkedIn ~3 lines (~200 characters) before the cut.
- **Authentic beats formulaic.** Audiences have real hook fatigue against obvious clickbait patterns ("You won't believe...", "Here's what I learned..."). The best-performing hooks in 2026 read like a genuine thought, not an engineered headline — bold and specific, not gimmicky.
- **One CTA per post.** Pick a single action (comment, save, click, DM) rather than asking for several at once — competing asks convert worse than one clear one.
- **Never post identical copy across platforms.** Each platform's algorithm and audience norms are different enough that cross-posted content reads as lazy and tends to get suppressed. Adapt the structure, not just the length — see the platform-specific reference files.
- **Specificity signals credibility.** A precise number ("6.60%," "$8,547," "22 lbs") reads as more real than a round one ("about 7%," "a lot of money") — and only ever use numbers that are actually true.

## Universal hook formulas

These work across virtually every platform — pick the one that fits the message, not the platform:

| Formula | Mechanism | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Curiosity Gap** | Reveal enough to intrigue, withhold the key detail | Any platform; must resolve honestly in the body or it reads as clickbait |
| **Contrarian/Bold Claim** | Challenge conventional wisdom to force a reaction | LinkedIn/Threads especially — debate drives engagement, but the body must actually defend the claim |
| **Warning-Style** | Alert to a specific mistake or risk (loss aversion) | Professional/B2B audiences; "one mistake" beats "mistakes" — specificity over generality |
| **Data-Driven** | Lead with one precise, real number | LinkedIn, threads, newsletters — anchors credibility immediately |
| **Question** | Mirror what the audience is already silently thinking | Any platform; use exactly one question, and frame it around the reader's desired outcome, not the topic |

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Posting the exact same text on every platform — adapt structure, not just trim length.
- Putting external links in the main post body (Twitter/X gives near-zero reach to link posts; LinkedIn suppresses them too) — links belong in the first comment, bio, or a link sticker instead.
- Over-hashtagging — diminishing returns fast, and it reads as spam past a handful. Roughly 3-5 on Instagram/LinkedIn, 0-2 on X/Threads.
- Generic, formulaic hooks ("Here's what I learned...") — specificity beats formula, especially in 2026's hook-fatigued feeds.
- Publishing without a human read-through — AI-flavored voice is detectable and erodes trust; always pass a final tone check.
- Skipping polls/interactive formats — often the highest-engagement format available and trivial to create, yet routinely overlooked.

## Before delivering, check:

- [ ] The hook works standalone, before any "see more" fold, for the target platform
- [ ] Formatting is mobile-first: short paragraphs, real line breaks
- [ ] Exactly one CTA, matched to the stated goal
- [ ] Any stat or claim is real (asked for, not invented)
- [ ] Tone matches the requested brand voice, adapted (not copy-pasted) for this specific platform
- [ ] If this is a repurposed piece, it reads as native to the platform, not a trimmed copy of the source

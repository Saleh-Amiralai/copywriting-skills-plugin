# Funnel-Stage Playbook (TOF / MOF / BOF)

The same ad angle can flop or convert depending entirely on how warm the audience already is. Use this to pick the right copy strategy, tone, and CTA style once the audience temperature is known.

## Top of funnel (TOF) — cold prospecting

**Who this reaches:** people who likely have never heard of the business.

**Objective:** generate awareness and interest — not, usually, an immediate sale, especially for a higher-ticket offer.

**Copy strategy:** problem/solution and education. Empathize with a broad, common pain or goal to hook anyone who has it, and introduce the solution *lightly* — offering value first (a guide, video, or quiz) rather than pitching a purchase outright. The job of a TOF ad is to sell the click, not the product.

**Tone:** educational and story-driven, not salesy. This is about building goodwill and authority, not closing.

**Example shape:** a life coach might offer a free "5 quick habits" checklist, with copy that names how hard it is to find time for self-care, rather than pitching a paid program directly. A consultant might promote a short "3 mistakes" video with a hook naming the mistake pattern, inviting the click as the only ask.

**What to measure:** click-through rate and cost per lead — conversion typically happens downstream, not on this ad. Think of TOF as a first date: make a strong impression, don't propose in the first message.

## Middle of funnel (MOF) — warm engagement and nurture

**Who this reaches:** people who've interacted before (visited the site, watched a video, clicked a TOF ad, joined the list) but haven't bought.

**Objective:** build trust, deepen the relationship, move them measurably closer to a decision.

**Copy strategy:** trust-building, objection-handling, and proof. This is the natural home for testimonials and case studies in retargeting — "still on the fence? see how \[client\] achieved \[result\]." Address the most likely objections directly but gently: "worried you're too new, or won't have time? that's exactly why we built this in 15-minute daily lessons."

**Tone:** encouraging and confidence-building — the underlying message is "we've got you, this works, you don't need to hesitate further."

**Example shape:** retargeting someone who visited a sales page but didn't buy, with an ad that leads with a specific client result and a closing-soon note — pairing social proof with a light nudge rather than a hard close.

## Bottom of funnel (BOF) — hot, ready to convert

**Who this reaches:** the strongest-intent group — cart abandoners, pricing-page visitors, people who've consumed a lot of content but haven't converted.

**Objective:** get the conversion. This audience already knows the offer; they need the final push.

**Copy strategy:** direct CTA, final objection-handling, and an incentive to act now. It's appropriate to be very straightforward here — "Enroll now," "Claim your spot" — because nurture has already happened elsewhere in the funnel. A limited-time bonus, a reminder of the guarantee, or a personalized nudge ("we saved your cart — finish by midnight for a bonus session") all work well.

**Tone:** confident and urgent, but still positive — frame it as "you're about to miss something great," not "I'm about to miss a sale."

**Example shape:** "You saw how \[name\] doubled their income. Don't let another quarter slip by — last chance to join \[program\]." Combine social proof (someone else already succeeded) with real urgency (a genuine deadline).

**Practical note:** BOF audiences are usually the smallest in volume but convert at the highest rate, since the targeting itself (website custom audiences, video viewers, lead-form openers) already filters for intent.

## Structuring the full sequence

Most 7- and 8-figure ad-driven businesses build a genuine TOF → MOF → BOF sequence rather than trying to do everything in a single ad: TOF content (lead magnets, short videos) generates and warms cold traffic, MOF retargets engagement with proof and objection-handling, BOF retargets the people who showed intent but didn't buy with a direct close. This keeps messaging relevant at every stage — new people aren't scared off by a hard sell, and hot leads aren't bored by a basic intro.

That said, treat this as a strong default, not a rigid rule — some advertisers successfully run one well-built ad (especially a Power Offer-style ad) straight to a cold audience and let the platform's delivery optimization find buyers, effectively skipping straight to a BOF-style pitch for the readiest 5-10% of the market. Test both approaches; let the data decide which structure a given audience actually needs.

**Match funnel depth to sales-cycle length.** A $50 impulse-buy digital product might only need TOF → BOF with a single retargeting step. A $5,000 coaching package usually needs the full sequence: TOF ad → lead magnet → nurture emails → MOF ad to a webinar → BOF ad to a booked call. Personal, high-trust niches (health, life coaching) typically need a heavier MOF phase; B2B niches often lean on more educational TOF (case studies, whitepapers) and a sharply ROI-focused BOF ("book a call to see how this adds $X to your bottom line").

**Keep the sequence coherent.** People often see ads from multiple stages around the same time as they move through the funnel — that's fine as long as the messages complement rather than contradict each other, and the core promise stays consistent even as the angle shifts. Watch frequency: if someone's seen the same BOF ad many times without converting, rotate in a new angle or incentive rather than repeating it into fatigue.

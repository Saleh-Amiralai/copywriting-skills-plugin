# Ad Copywriter

This skill turns Claude into a Meta ads copywriter for digital products, courses, and coaching offers. Ad copy is a distinct discipline from email or landing-page copy: it has to earn attention from a genuinely cold, scrolling audience in the first line, work within platform truncation limits, and match its persuasion angle to how warm the specific audience already is toward the offer.

## Before writing: get context

Ask for what's missing (skip what's already established in the conversation):

- **The offer**: what's being sold or given away, at what price, and what specific transformation it delivers.
- **The audience temperature**: is this ad going to a cold audience who's never heard of the business (prospecting), a warm audience who's engaged before but hasn't bought (retargeting/nurture), or a hot audience who's shown strong buying intent (cart abandoners, pricing-page visitors)? This determines both the framework and the funnel-stage approach below.
- **Where the click goes**: a lead magnet, a webinar registration, a sales page, a DM/comment funnel? The ad's job is to sell the *click*, not necessarily the end product — know what that next step actually is.
- **Brand voice**: default to conversational and clear; ask (or infer from prior context/examples) whether the brand leans bold/edgy, warm/empathetic, or polished/professional — tone should fit the audience and niche, not a generic assumption.
- **Available proof**: testimonials, specific results/numbers, credentials, media mentions — anything on hand to make claims credible rather than just asserted.

## Ad copy structure and style

- **The hook is everything.** The first 1-3 lines must stop the scroll — a bold claim, a sharp question, a surprising statement. Meta truncates text on mobile after roughly the first 125 characters (before "See More"), so the single strongest line goes first, not buried in a preamble.
- **Write conversationally, at eye level.** Aim for a middle-school reading level, short sentences, and language the audience actually uses about their own problem — mirror their words rather than internal jargon. "We help you come up with business ideas" beats "Our program facilitates entrepreneurial ideation." Tone still adapts to niche: warmer and more personal for B2C wellness/life coaching, professionally conversational for B2B/executive audiences — but clarity and empathy always outperform sounding impressive.
- **Sell the transformation, not the features.** Acknowledge the problem, then paint a vivid, specific "after" — more revenue, more time, more confidence, whatever the offer actually delivers. Tie every feature back to what it means for the reader.
- **Use storytelling when it earns its place** — especially for higher-ticket offers or cold audiences, where a brief, relevant, credible narrative lowers skepticism faster than a features list. Keep it tight: only the details that build toward the payoff.
- **Match length to funnel stage and offer complexity.** Short copy (1-2 tight paragraphs) suits retargeting or simple/low-ticket offers — quick value, clear CTA, no over-explaining. Long-form copy (several paragraphs, sometimes 200+ words) suits cold traffic for complex or high-ticket offers, where there's room to build trust, address doubts, and create desire within the ad itself. When writing long copy, every sentence has to earn its place — pad and it reads as filler. Break text into short paragraphs or line breaks for scannability; heavy walls of text lose readers regardless of how good the words are.

## Copy frameworks

Pick one as the backbone, based on audience temperature and offer type — details and full sample ads for each are in [references/frameworks-and-examples.md](references/frameworks-and-examples.md):

| Framework | Shape | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Hook-Story-Offer** | Hook → 3-5 sentence story (yours or a client's) → offer with clear value + CTA | General-purpose; works especially well for cold traffic on higher-ticket offers |
| **Problem-Agitate-Solution (PAS)** | Name the problem → deepen the felt pain → present the offer as relief | Pain-aware audiences who already feel the problem acutely |
| **Before-After-Bridge** | Vivid "before" state → vivid "after" state → offer as the bridge between them | Selling a transformation; scale the contrast to match how dramatic the real change is |
| **Power Offer** (Joel Erway) | One or two sentences: "If I offered to \[result\] without \[usual pain\], would you \[action\]?" | The most ready 5-10% of a market; skips nurture entirely, needs a genuinely compelling, provable claim |
| **Tried-and-Failed Market** | Name what they've already tried and failed at → agitate the fatigue of that → introduce a new mechanism as the differentiator | "Wounded buyers" who are skeptical and burned out from prior attempts, not first-timers |
| **Us/Them (New Way)** | Old way (pain) → new way (promise) → the mechanism → belonging ("what the smart few are doing") | Saturated niches with an obvious default method to contrast against |
| **Why Me** | Avatar + their problem → why it matters now → why this specific person/offer → proof → offer + CTA | Warm, comparison-shopping audiences (middle-of-funnel) who are already evaluating solutions, not discovering the problem |

## Matching copy to funnel stage

Ad copy's effectiveness depends heavily on who's actually seeing it — the same framework can flop at the wrong temperature. Full depth (tone notes, example angles, campaign-structure guidance) lives in [references/funnel-stage-playbook.md](references/funnel-stage-playbook.md); the short version:

- **Top of funnel (cold, prospecting)** — sell the click, not the product. Lead with a broad, relatable pain or goal, offer something light and valuable (a guide, a video, a quiz), and keep the tone educational rather than salesy. This is a first date, not a proposal.
- **Middle of funnel (warm, has engaged but hasn't bought)** — build trust and handle objections directly. This is where testimonials, case studies, and "still on the fence?" retargeting earns its keep. Be more direct about the offer since the audience already knows who's speaking.
- **Bottom of funnel (hot, strong intent, hasn't converted)** — be straightforward: a clear CTA, a final incentive or urgency element, and a reminder of what they're about to miss. These audiences are smallest but convert highest, so it's fine to be specific and direct.

Match funnel depth to sales-cycle length: a low-ticket impulse buy can go straight from cold ad to purchase; a $5,000 coaching package usually needs the full TOF → MOF → BOF sequence.

## Psychological triggers

Weave these in deliberately rather than relying on the framework alone to carry persuasion — full detail, including trust-building and social-proof placement, is in [references/psychological-triggers-and-trust.md](references/psychological-triggers-and-trust.md):

- **Scarcity/urgency** — a real deadline or limited capacity taps loss aversion, which motivates more than an equivalent potential gain.
- **Social proof** — testimonials, numbers, ratings; people trust that "someone like me already succeeded" more than any direct claim.
- **Authority** — credentials, results, media mentions, reduce perceived risk by putting a credible source behind the promise.
- **Emotional resonance** — name the real pain or paint the real aspiration; people decide on feeling and justify with logic afterward.
- **Reciprocity** — genuinely useful value delivered in the ad itself (a tip, a free resource) creates goodwill that carries into the ask.

All of these only work when they're true. Fabricated scarcity, invented testimonials, or borrowed authority that doesn't hold up are not just unethical — they're detectable, and once noticed they destroy trust permanently.

## Meta-specific guardrails

- **Never phrase personal-attribute callouts accusatorially.** "You have debt? You need this!" reads as a personal attack and risks policy rejection — reframe as a compassionate observation instead: "Struggling with debt can feel overwhelming…"
- **The ad's promise must match the landing page exactly.** If the ad says "free training," the next page can't immediately hard-pitch a paid product — any bait-and-switch feel destroys trust and hurts account performance over time.
- **Scarcity and urgency must be real** — no countdowns that silently reset, no fabricated "spots left."
- **Testimonials need real names (or explicit permission)** and should feel specific and attributable, not generic or invented.

## Before delivering ad copy, check:

- [ ] The first line alone would stop a scroll — it doesn't rely on the rest of the ad to earn attention
- [ ] Copy is pitched at the actual funnel stage (cold vs. warm vs. hot), not a generic pitch
- [ ] At least one psychological trigger is present and genuinely true, not just asserted
- [ ] Language mirrors how the audience actually talks about their problem, not internal/brand jargon
- [ ] There's a single, clear CTA
- [ ] Length matches the offer's complexity and the audience's temperature (short for warm/simple, long-form only when cold + complex justifies it)
- [ ] Nothing promised in the ad would surprise someone on the very next page
